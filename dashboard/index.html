Background

The Study

Burnout and unmet needs don't just vary across employees; they fluctuate within the same employee across the workday. This study asks: do those within-day fluctuations predict when employees are most likely to think about quitting?

Conceptual model. Variables measured at three points per workday (L1, within-person) are shown above the dashed line; stable employee characteristics (L2, between-person) below. The outcome is turnover intentions at each measurement point.

Why does this matter? Most research on burnout and turnover measures employees once or twice. Measuring three times daily reveals how the workday itself shapes withdrawal thoughts, and which employees are most at risk, hour by hour.

Study design

Three-wave experience sampling (ESM) design: participants completed surveys at the beginning, middle, and end of their shift on each workday for approximately two weeks. Multilevel modeling (HLM) with random slopes partitions variance into within-person (L1) and between-person (L2) components. Primary outcome: turnover intentions assessed at every time point.

Intraclass correlation (ICC)

ICC = between-person variance / total variance. ICC = .792 for turnover intentions, estimated from the unconditional means model (M0). High ICC indicates stable trait-like differences; the remaining within-person variance (21%) is the portion the L1 predictors can explain.

What it means

Takeaways

The findings reframe turnover intentions: withdrawal cognition is not only something employees have; it is something their workday does to them, hour by hour.

Practical implication: Within-day burnout signals (fatigue, exhaustion, competence threat) function as leading indicators of withdrawal cognition. Interventions that reduce daily load spikes may attenuate turnover risk beyond what trait-level assessments can detect.
Three qualified findings:
  • PC Breach was supported at p = .050 (boundary significance). Effect is directionally consistent but should be replicated.
  • Cognitive weariness (between-person) showed a negative coefficient, contrary to the predicted direction. CW and EE are highly correlated at L2; suppression likely inflates the CW estimate.
  • Meeting load moderation (H3a, H3b) was not supported. Meeting count and time did not amplify within-person effects, possibly due to floor effects (knowledge workers with moderate meeting loads).

Study limitations

Power simulations indicated approximately 42% power for the smallest targeted within-person effect sizes (f2 ~ .02). The sample skews toward knowledge workers, limiting generalizability to manual or service-sector roles. Three daily measurement occasions may not capture faster within-day dynamics. Cross-sectional between-person associations cannot establish causality.

Scope & Constraints

Limitations

Seven constraints qualify these findings. They do not invalidate the conclusions but specify where replication, densification, and broader sampling are needed before generalizing further.

Statistical Power
42% of target enrollment
N = 336 against an a priori target of 800. Full power is achieved for medium and larger effects; power drops to approximately .62 to .84 for the PC breach and competence frustration findings specifically. Those two results carry provisional framing accordingly.
Between-person need frustration nulls are undetermined
The study was not powered to detect small between-person effects. Null results for autonomy and relatedness frustration at L2 should be read as undetermined rather than established absences until a fully powered replication is available.
Study Design
Three occasions per day
With only three measurement points, random slope variance is downwardly biased and the intraday trajectory can only be modeled as linear drift. Five or more occasions would unlock stable slope estimation and non-linear trajectory characterization.
Survivorship in the analytic sample
Only participants who completed all three occasions were included. Momentary states that drive same-day dropout are unobservable, placing a survivorship boundary on the within-day inferences.
Measurement
Single-item turnover intentions criterion
A single item precludes internal consistency estimation and within-occasion momentary retest reliability. Measurement error in the criterion attenuates observed coefficients toward zero, meaning the reported effects are conservative.
Two documented administration errors
One physical fatigue item was lost to a survey administration error. Job insecurity and desirability of movement entered as post hoc controls rather than pre-specified covariates.
Generalizability
Knowledge-worker skew
The sample skews educated and majority-remote. The within-day findings may not generalize to hourly, frontline, or manual occupations where depletion mechanisms and exit costs differ substantially from knowledge work.
Recruitment source as main effect only
Snowball and CloudResearch participants were controlled via a main effect in the between-person model. Homogeneity of predictor slopes across sources is an assumption, not a tested finding.
Analytical Approach
Multilevel linear regression applied to an ordinal, skewed outcome
Turnover intentions were measured on a 1-to-5 frequency scale anchored "Never" to "Very often." Psychological scaling research consistently finds that verbal frequency anchors are not perceived as equally spaced: the gap between "Never" and "Rarely" is not the same as the gap between "Often" and "Very often" in psychological units. In this sample, the outcome had a mean of 1.57 (out of 5.0) and a skewness of 2.14, placing the bulk of responses at the floor with a long right tail. Multilevel linear regression assumes an interval-level, continuous, and unbounded outcome with normally distributed residuals; none of those assumptions hold here.

The principled alternative is multilevel ordinal regression via cumulative link mixed models (CLMM). Rather than estimating unit differences in a continuous score, CLMMs model the probability of exceeding each response threshold (1 to 2, 2 to 3, etc.) without requiring equal spacing between categories. They respect the bounded 1-to-5 range, handle floor and ceiling compression without violating residual normality, and produce threshold parameters that are more directly interpretable for ordered categorical outcomes.

The overall pattern of findings reported here is likely robust to this distinction; linear and ordinal approaches tend to agree on direction and significance when effects are not extreme and the scale has five or more categories. However, the specific coefficient magnitudes and their standard errors are best interpreted as approximations. A CLMM reanalysis is the most targeted methodological extension for this line of work.

Future Directions

These constraints define the natural next steps.

01
Densify to 5+ occasions per day
More measurement points across multiple days would enable stable random slope estimation, formal moderation tests, and characterization of the intraday trajectory beyond the linear drift recoverable here.
02
Retest meeting moderation at scale
Managers and high-meeting knowledge workers provide the meeting exposure needed for a fair test. Facet-specific predictors (fatigue, exhaustion) rather than a composite would sharpen the hypothesis.
03
Separate breach from violation by design
The current data cannot adjudicate between breach (cognitive appraisal) and violation (affective reaction). Temporal ordering or experimental vignettes can separate the two components cleanly.
04
Test need frustration mediation paths
Autonomy and relatedness frustration may reach withdrawal through accumulated burnout rather than directly. Mediation designs would test this and reconcile the present nulls with the longitudinal literature.
